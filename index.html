DECISION SUPPORT FOR UK ADULT SOCIAL CARE

Spot changes in a resident’s usual routine earlier.

CareSense360 is being developed to bring care notes, medication records and staffing information together. When observations across shifts suggest a change in a resident’s usual pattern, the platform shows the care team what changed and why it warrants review. A care professional decides what happens next.

COMPATIBLE WITH EXISTING SYSTEMS

Working alongside care-home records

CareSense360 is designed to work alongside the care-planning, medication and staffing systems a home already uses. Its purpose is to bring relevant observations together, explain emerging changes and help staff follow an agreed action through to its outcome.

MODULAR CAPABILITIES

What the platform will do

A staged, human-centred development roadmap designed specifically for UK care-home workflows.

Year 1 · Initial pilot CORE PLATFORM SPECIFICATION

The three foundation modules deployed for the initial care-home pilot.

01 INITIAL PILOT

Data Intake and Normalisation

Brings available care-planning records, electronic medication administration records (eMAR), rostering information and available sensor records into a common format. Checks data quality so observations can be compared across systems and shifts.

02 INITIAL PILOT

Resident Baseline and Risk Engine

Builds a picture of each resident’s usual food intake, sleep, mobility and behaviour. Looks for related changes over time and shows the observations that prompted a concern.

03 INITIAL PILOT

Human Validation and Task Workflow

Routes a recommendation to the appropriate care professional. Staff can accept, modify, reject or escalate it. Agreed actions become assigned tasks, with the decision and outcome recorded.

Year 2 · Planned expansion
04 PLANNED EXPANSION

Smart Inventory and Procurement

Examines consumption of medicines, food and care supplies. Flags unusual usage or possible wastage and recommends purchasing actions for staff approval.

05 PLANNED EXPANSION

Maintenance and Compliance Evidence

Supports preventive maintenance scheduling and helps managers organise records and evidence for Care Quality Commission (CQC) inspections.

Year 3 · Planned expansion
06 PLANNED EXPANSION

Family Portal and Outcome Reporting

Gives families access to information appropriate to share and gives operators reporting on recorded outcomes.

DECISION ARCHITECTURE

From a care note to a reviewed action

A transparent five-step sequence keeping registered managers and senior carers in complete control of clinical decisions.

1

Connect existing records

The home identifies its available sources. Relevant care, medication, staffing and available sensor records are brought into a common format and checked for quality.

2

Learn each resident’s usual pattern

An initial baseline is expected to take approximately two to four weeks of observations. Timing depends on how frequently and completely the home records information.

3

Identify and explain a change

The platform compares recent observations with the resident’s baseline and shows the evidence behind a concern. Display the four stages in this exact order:

  1. Normal
  2. →
  3. Emerging Change
  4. →
  5. Increasing Risk
  6. →
  7. High Concern
4

Ask a care professional to review

The appropriate staff member examines the evidence and can accept, modify, reject or escalate the recommendation. The planned workflow includes a separate pathway for time-critical safety events.

5

Record the action and its outcome

Staff assign and track an agreed action, then record what happened. An ordinary explanation or false alarm is also valuable feedback. Recorded outcomes help refine future alerts for that resident and leave an audit trail across shifts.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Everything care operators need to know about the planned platform and pilot.

Can we keep our current care-planning system?

CareSense360 is designed to work alongside a home’s existing systems. During onboarding, the team assesses which care records, medication systems, rostering tools and available sensor feeds can be connected.

Who decides what happens after an alert?

A care professional reviews the evidence and decides whether to accept, modify, reject or escalate the recommendation. The decision and any resulting task are recorded.

How long does it take to learn a resident’s usual pattern?

The plan estimates approximately two to four weeks of observations for an initial baseline. Timing depends on the frequency and completeness of the resident’s records.

How is resident information expected to be protected?

The planned platform design includes UK data hosting, encryption, role-based access and audit logs. Access is intended to reflect each staff member’s responsibilities within the home.

What is included in the initial pilot?

The pilot focuses on connecting existing records, learning resident baselines, explaining emerging changes and supporting staff review. Inventory, maintenance and family-facing tools are planned for later phases.

Who is the initial pilot aimed at?

The plan targets two to three West Midlands residential or nursing care homes that already use digital care records and are willing to provide feedback during development.

How much would the pilot cost for a 40-bed home?

At the indicative pilot rate of approximately £8 per bed per month, the core subscription would be approximately £320 per month for 40 beds, before any agreed onboarding fee.
